01What's covered

Zone by zone.

A fit-out can be one bait board or the whole boat. This is what we build in, working from the transom forward.

Zone 01

Stern

Bait boards & cutting stations
A board at the right height, with somewhere for the knives, the bait and the mess to go.
Live bait tanks & plumbing
Tanks, pumps and plumbing set up to keep bait alive and to drain where they should.
Battery boxes & hidden cabling
Batteries secured and cabling run out of sight, so nothing is underfoot or sitting in the bilge.

Zone 02

Deck

Rod holders & rod storage
Holders and racks placed where they are in reach and out of the way, and fixed so they stay put.
Seating & pedestals
Seats and pedestals mounted solidly, at a height and position that suits the person using them.

Zone 03

Helm

Dash & console layouts
Screens, switches and gauges arranged so you can see and reach them, with a clean panel behind.
Wired as part of the job
New screens, lights and pumps get a proper fused supply, run with the rest of the 12V work.

Zone 04

Bow

Casting decks & hatches
Raised decks with dry storage underneath, and hatches that open without everything being moved first.
Electric motor bow mounts
A mount, plug and heavy cabling for a bow-mount electric, with the battery where the weight suits the boat.

02The thinking

What makes a layout work.

Bolting gear on is the easy part. Four things decide whether it is still a pleasure to use a season later.

  • /01

    Reach

    The things you grab most sit where your hands already are. Rods, pliers and the bait board shouldn't need a walk across the deck.

  • /02

    Weight

    Batteries, tanks and eskies change how a small boat sits and rides, so the heavy items are placed with trim in mind.

  • /03

    Water

    Everything that gets wet has to drain. Decks, hatches and tanks are set up so water leaves the boat and stays away from the wiring.

  • /04

    Wiring

    Cable runs are planned before any holes are cut, so new gear gets a fused supply and nothing is left dangling.
